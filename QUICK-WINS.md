## Why this exists

The B52–B57 pass tested for *recurrence over time*: a pattern had to appear on two or more
separate days to qualify, and the older it was the more repetitions it needed. That is the
right test for structural builds, and the wrong test for everything asked for once, last
Tuesday, by someone who needs it now.

Re-mining the same corpus for **concrete asks regardless of recurrence** returned **118
build requests in 75 days**. Below is the subset that is small, owned, and startable — most
in days rather than weeks, and several are *already-built things pointed at a new target*.

**Sizing:** ▲ = days · ● = 1–2 weeks. Nothing here is a platform.

## What I deliberately left out

Roughly 80 of the 118 asks are **platform-scale or deal-side**, not quick wins:
governance-OS platforms, core-banking architecture, middleware and integration builds,
capital-formation and fund-structuring work, market-entry SOPs for other geographies.
Real, but none of them are days of work, and most are not internal-operations automation
at all. They are listed here as a category so nobody assumes they were missed.

A further small set touches confidential organisational and governance matters. Those
surfaced in the mining and are deliberately not characterised here; they sit with Logan and
Bunmi directly.

---

## The honest caveat

These are **single mentions**, by design. That means each one carries a risk the B52–B57 set
does not: it may already be done, already assigned, or have been a passing thought in a
meeting. Every item above should be confirmed with its named owner before it is scoped —
which for most of them is one message, not a meeting.

The trade is deliberate. The structural list is high-confidence and slow. This list is
lower-confidence and fast. **Run both.**
